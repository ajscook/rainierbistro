A few things that stood out

  • The neighbors keep restaurant hours about like yours. The difference is a market or grab-and-go case that covers every hour in between.
  • Across the hotel industry, grab-and-go keeps growing. One Hyatt executive reports room service at his 275-room hotel fell to about 10% of grab-and-go sales over four years.
  • Even Hotels lets guests order a meal at night and pick it up on their way out in the morning.

Grab and go opportunities

Early and late guests

What's working

Breakfast is cooked to order, and a guest staying the night before a cruise called it "very good."

Where it could go

A lot of your guests are out the door before 7 and coming in after 9. Some arrive with food vouchers and have found the bistro closed. A stocked case meets them on their schedule instead of the bistro's.

Labor

What's working

You already have a crew on the clock for breakfast and dinner, and a front desk staffed through the night.

Where it could go

Prepping and keeping up a par of grab-and-go gives your crew productive work in the slow stretches of a shift. Once it's stocked, it can sell 24/7 without a cook, and the front desk can ring it up.

Price

What's working

Guests already name the BBQ bacon cheeseburger, the grilled club and the salads.

Where it could go

Price comes up in reviews, and the $15 breakfast gets called overpriced and limited. Packaged versions of the favorites, plus fruit and simple breakfast items, give guests a lighter option at a lighter price.

Parallel Techstack

You've signed up with Toast for the bistro, and it can run side by side with the hotel. I don't know how your systems are set up today, so I'm starting from a blank slate. Forgive anything that reads like an assumption. I don't have any. Here's how Toast could sit next to the hotel's system, and a few things worth talking through.

The HOTEL

Reservations and folios

  • SureStay hotels are required to run Best Western's own property management system, AutoClerk, for reservations and guest folios.
  • AutoClerk talks to other systems through a connector called Lodging Link, from a company called Comtrol, which usually runs on a dedicated PC on site.

The BISTRO

Toast, alongside the hotel

  • Toast gives the bistro its own card processing, reports and online ordering, separate from the hotel. Best Western named Toast an endorsed POS provider for its hotels in August 2026.
  • Toast also offers a direct connection to AutoClerk as a paid add-on, so room charges can post to guest folios if you want them to. The setup below works with or without it.
